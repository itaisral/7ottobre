7 October 2023

“An act of resistance”

University of Bologna • 7 October 2026

On 7 October 2026, the third anniversary of the massacre, the group Giovani Palestinesi (Young Palestinians) filled a lecture hall of the Faculty of Philology at the University of Bologna, the Aula Capecchi, for a screening of the film Palestine 36 followed by a debate. The event took place without the university's authorisation.

Before the film, speaking into the microphone, one of the organisers called 7 October “an important operation of the Palestinian resistance”, spoke of “marginal episodes that were wrong” and compared the attack to the partisan action in Via Rasella.

Bologna, 7 October 2026. A massacre called resistance

Listen to this article7 min · synthetic voice

On 7 October this year, while Israel and Jewish communities across half the world were remembering the dead of the worst massacre of Jews since the Shoah, that massacre was being celebrated in a lecture hall of the University of Bologna. We have transcribed word for word what was said at the microphone and published it with subtitles in English, Hebrew and Arabic, so that nobody can come and tell us we misunderstood. We condemn it without any mitigation. And since the people we are answering are so fond of history, let us take their arguments one at a time.

It begins with 7 October described as “an important operation of the Palestinian resistance”. That day a few thousand men from Hamas and other armed groups broke through the border and slaughtered about twelve hundred people, almost all of them civilians. At the Nova festival they killed more than three hundred and sixty young people who were dancing. In Be'eri, Kfar Aza and Nir Oz they went into homes, wiped out entire families and burned some of them alive. Two hundred and fifty-one people, from newborn babies to eighty-five-year-olds, were carried off to Gaza as spoils. This is supposed to be the “operation”. It is the word Hamas uses in its communiqués, and we do not believe that is a coincidence.

Then comes the “concentration camp”, the “open-air prison” from which tens of thousands of people supposedly broke out by tearing down walls and fences. The speaker should be reminded that in 2005 Israel left Gaza, withdrew every soldier and evacuated the very last settler. That in 2007 Hamas seized power by killing its Fatah rivals. That the Israeli and Egyptian blockade came afterwards, while rockets were being fired from the Strip and tunnels were being dug with cement meant for housing. And that in Europe the concentration camps were called Auschwitz, Dachau, Fossoli. Using that word for Gaza, in Italy, inside a university, is an obscenity. As for the walls and fences, on the other side there were villages, kindergartens and a field full of young people. That is where they went, armed.

The massacres become “marginal episodes that were wrong”. Marginal. Human Rights Watch, which is no agency of the Israeli government, wrote in July 2024 that killing civilians and taking hostages were central aims of the attack, prepared long in advance, and spoke of war crimes and crimes against humanity. The UN Special Representative on Sexual Violence in Conflict found reasonable grounds to believe that rape, including gang rape, took place in several locations. The terrorists filmed themselves with cameras strapped to their chests, and one of them called his parents from a victim's phone to boast of how many Jews he had killed with his own hands. There is nothing marginal about any of this.

The most shameful comparison is the one with Via Rasella. On 23 March 1944 the GAP partisans struck a column of the German police regiment Bozen, soldiers of an occupying army, in an occupied Rome. The civilians killed in the explosion, among them little Piero Zuccheretti, are still the open wound of that story, because nobody had made them a target. On 7 October those were exactly the people they went looking for, room by room, in shelters, under beds. To those in Bologna who play at being partisans, we would also point out that in the spring of 1945, on the Senio, the Jewish Brigade was among those liberating Italy, five thousand Jewish volunteers with the Star of David on their sleeves, many of whom a few years later would defend the newborn State of Israel. Putting the partisans on the same level as Hamas, whose 1988 charter openly incites the killing of Jews, is an insult to them and to everyone who died for the freedom of this country.

Then there is the child. The speaker admits that a child died in Via Rasella, grants us his “regret” and adds that this, in any case, “cannot change the perspective”. Said in a speech about 7 October, when more than thirty children were murdered, it is a sentence that chills the blood. It means that no death, however small the victim, will make him change his mind. To us this is fanaticism, pure and simple.

Next comes the history lesson, ninety years of “British colonialism” that supposedly paved the way for “Zionist colonialism”, with the “great Arab revolt” as a model. It is half a story, the half that suits them. Jews have always lived in that land, and in Jerusalem they were already the majority in the nineteenth century. In 1939 the British all but closed the doors to Jewish immigration, just as Europe's Jews were desperately looking for somewhere to flee. The 1936 revolt was led by the Grand Mufti of Jerusalem, Amin al-Husseini, the same man who in 1941 went to Berlin to shake Hitler's hand and later recruited Muslims for the SS. That revolt killed hundreds of Jews and a great many Arabs, murdered by other Arabs. When in 1937 the Peel Commission proposed dividing the land into two states, the Arab leaders said no. Zionism is the national movement of a people that came home and brought its own language back to life there, and calling it colonialism is simply false.

We come to “ethnic cleansing” and “genocide”, words now repeated like a mantra. About two million Arab citizens live in Israel, a fifth of the population, and they vote and sit in the Knesset and on the Supreme Court. There are far more Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank today than in 1967. The Gaza war was started by Hamas on 7 October, and Hamas fought it hiding in homes, schools and hospitals, with the hostages locked in tunnels. No court has ever ruled that Israel has committed genocide. But the game is always the same, repeating the accusation until the victim of 7 October becomes the defendant.

Then there is the “Zionist entity”. Israel has been a member state of the United Nations since 1949, but these gentlemen will not even grant it a name. They talk like Hamas and like the ayatollahs in Tehran, who want Israel erased, and someone ought to ask why. They demand that the university break off all relations with Israeli universities, in other words that it punish scientists, doctors and students according to their passport, including the many Arabs who study and teach at those universities. In 1938 Italian universities expelled their Jewish professors. Someone in Bologna ought to remember that.

At the microphone the enemy also has a name, “Zionist”, and in Bologna those who recognise themselves in that word are first of all the city's Jews. According to the press, the organisers had not even asked for the lecture hall. They simply took it. Then they said they did not want the police to protect them and warned that, if there were any public order problems, the blame would lie with the university and the police. In plain words, either you let us do whatever we want, or whatever happens is your fault. It is blackmail, and not even thinly veiled.

Finally, the university as “a place of openness, democratic, of debate”. This from someone who, on the same day, had explained that no dead child can change his perspective, and that the only opponents he expected were “Zionists” and “provocateurs”. That day, in that hall, a massacre was celebrated on the anniversary of mourning, inside the oldest university in Europe. Freedom of speech protects them too, and rightly so. It also protects us when we say that what happened in Bologna is a disgrace.

The Associazione Italia Israele di Alessandria stands with the Jewish Community of Bologna and calls on the Rector of the University of Bologna to take a clear, public position on what happened in the lecture halls of his university.
