7 October 2023

“An act of resistance”

University of Bologna • 7 October 2026

On 7 October 2026, the third anniversary of the massacre, the group Giovani Palestinesi (Young Palestinians) filled a lecture hall of the Faculty of Philology at the University of Bologna, the Aula Capecchi, for a screening of the film Palestine 36 followed by a debate. The event took place without the university's authorisation.

Before the film, speaking into the microphone, one of the organisers called 7 October “an important operation of the Palestinian resistance”, spoke of “marginal episodes that were wrong” and compared the attack to the partisan action in Via Rasella.

In the lecture hall · 00:00–01:09

“October 7 is important for what it was, an important operation of the Palestinian resistance. It is important because on that day tens of thousands of people imprisoned for decades in a concentration camp, in an open-air prison, tore down walls, tore down fences. In all acts of resistance, in all acts of war carried out by human beings, there may have been some marginal episodes that were wrong, but we can compare October 7 to one of the most important partisan actions, for example of the Italian Resistance, like the one in Via Rasella, in which, as we know, there were civilian deaths, even a child. But this, even our regret over it, if you like, cannot change the perspective from which we analyse that event. That is an act of resistance.”

The interview · 01:17–03:50

“It is an introduction to the film screening, to the day. Then we screen the film and afterwards there will be a debate. Our aim is to place October 7, 2023 within the history of the Palestinian people and of the Palestinian resistance. It is a history that is ninety years old. […] the evidence of how British colonialism favoured the settlement of Zionist colonialism in Palestine. Just as ninety years ago the great Arab revolt broke out, today too the Palestinian people continue to resist occupation, ethnic cleansing and genocide. For us, October 7, 2023 is a date, a day within these ninety years.

We are talking about a university that for over three years we have asked to cut its ties with the Zionist entity, and instead, despite three years of genocide, it keeps up its military and economic relations with Israel, it keeps contributing to the production of military equipment which then fuels the genocide in Gaza, it keeps making new agreements with Israel. We are not surprised that today, too, it took the wrong side of history.

We were here today to hold the event, we were not afraid of anyone, of any Zionist, of any character who probably makes provocation his job. So clearly we do not let ourselves be protected by the police, nor do we let ourselves be protected by those who are campaigning for election. In case of provocations we would easily have been able to handle them without giving in to any kind of provocation. If the university and the police intend to turn this day into a public order problem, they will bear the responsibility. We are here to hold an entirely peaceful event, a film screening and a debate, and I think it would be a serious precedent, especially in a setting like the university, which should be a place of openness, democratic, of debate, if it went as far as using the police to prevent it.”

Via Rasella

On 23 March 1944, in Rome, partisans of the GAP detonated a bomb as a column of the German police regiment Bozen marched past, killing 33 of its men. Some civilians also died in the explosion, among them a young boy, Piero Zuccheretti. The next day, in reprisal, the Nazis shot 335 people at the Fosse Ardeatine.
